03 / CLIMATE → PRECIPITATION RESEARCH

The historical relationship atlas

2004–2025 · preserved Phase 4B / Phase 7B research

RETROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATION

What counts as an extreme day? The selected daily precipitation metric is at or above its regional, seasonal 95th percentile in the 1991–2020 reference. The atlas shows historical frequencies for 2004–2025.

Target definitions & how to read the numbers
Two ways a daily precipitation field can qualify
Selected targetDefinition of an extreme day
Regional mean extremesThe daily regional mean precipitation reaches or exceeds the 95th percentile of daily regional means for that region and season.
Extreme coverage of ≥25 mmThe fraction of the region receiving at least 25 mm of daily precipitation reaches or exceeds the 95th percentile of that daily area fraction for the region and season.

25 mm (about 1 inch) defines the rainfall coverage being measured. The area fraction must also reach its seasonal 95th-percentile threshold to make the day extreme. Percentiles use all valid reference days, including dry days; thresholds vary by region and season.

RONI / ENSO bars
Extreme days per 100 valid days. For example, 8 means 8% of the valid historical days in that climate state met the selected extreme-day definition. The readout also gives the underlying day counts.
PNA / NAO curves
Percent of historical response windows containing at least one qualifying extreme day. “Same ending day” is a single day; Days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21 and 22–28 are seven-day windows after the climate-driver anchor date.
MJO phase-space colors
Smoothed historical extreme-day/window frequency minus the selected population’s baseline, in percentage points. For example, 15% versus a 10% baseline is +5 percentage points. Hatching marks insufficient sample support or excluded weak states.
Season and counting
DJF = December–February; MAM = March–May; JJA = June–August; SON = September–November. The anchor season groups climate-driver dates. A response window can cross a season boundary; each precipitation day uses its own season’s threshold. A prolonged storm can contribute multiple extreme days and overlapping windows.

These are precipitation days or windows, rather than unique storm totals. They are not counts of Flash Flood Warnings or Local Storm Reports. “Extreme” is a climatological research definition, not a flash-flood threshold or an issued forecast.
